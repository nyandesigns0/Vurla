# Vurla design system

## Overview

Vurla creates spaces and objects with intention. This fixed dark architectural system places original work on a night ground with hard-edged panels, fine structural rules, restrained signal color, and generous editorial space. It retains Vurla's own brand, authored content, and project imagery; there is no user-selectable theme.

## Colors

Six physical primitives define the current system: night (`#121212`), graphite (`#505050`), mineral (`#C4BEB1`), paper (`#F4F3F0`), red (`#B22D18`), and blue (`#33539C`). Night is the page and chapter ground; paper is primary text and a local reading surface; mineral is muted text and a secondary light panel; graphite is structure and a secondary dark panel. Red is the primary action/current/error signal and blue is the hover/success/spatial signal. Semantic roles reference these six primitive names. Compatibility role names map to the same primitives while consumer migration proceeds; no legacy purple, lime or light-only palette remains in canonical tokens. Original project photography stays recognizable and naturally colored except for intentional resting-state grayscale treatments that restore color on interaction.

Use on-surface for text on neutral surfaces and on-primary on primary actions. Error messages use error-text on error-container: the supplied error red is an accent, not small text on the page surface. Outline is a control boundary; outline-variant is decorative. Check every pairing at 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large type and required boundaries. The guide measures potential on-fill contrast against each actual swatch. Copy-button labels sit outside the color patch on a compliant neutral surface. Field placeholders use opaque muted text, never a half-opacity inherited color.

## Typography

Share Tech Mono 400 owns display headings, navigation, buttons, captions, and metadata. Chakra Petch 400 owns body copy and form text. Display references 60/68px, large headings 44/52px, medium headings 32/40px, card titles 24/32px; body references 18/28px and 16/24px. UI labels use 14/20px and metadata 12/16px. Responsive clamps preserve broad readable headlines; never rewrite copy to make it fit. The home headline uses a 2.05rem compact size below 768px and retains its authored wording. Guide headings show full desktop token metrics; below 768px their specimens cap at 32px with 1.2 line height, stated alongside the specimens.

## Layout

The shell is at most 1280px with 24px gutters. A twelve-column desktop grid supports asymmetric compositions, while content stacks on narrow screens. Base spacing is 4, 12, 24, 40, and 64px; section intervals extend to 96 and 128px. Preserve natural document scrolling and reflow at 200% zoom. `SiteShell` owns one header, one main landmark, and one footer across visitor routes. On the homepage it supplies the header and footer as server-authored slots consumed once by Chapters 1 and 4; on ordinary routes they remain outside main in conventional document order. The homepage contains exactly four chapters: hero and full-width statistics, Services, Selected Projects, and New Work with the single shared footer.

The spatial hero reserves its height and renders its four joined corridor surfaces with a bounded Three.js WebGL renderer. Project images occupy fixed room-grid positions, including two continuous ceiling-to-wall folds and one floor-to-wall fold; each pair shares hinge vertices, continuous UVs, one source crop, and one interaction identity. Semantic projected project links remain available independently of the canvas. The full-width four-metric statistics bar ends Chapter 1 and must not move during hero interaction. Compact, coarse-pointer, and reduced-motion modes retain a stationary Three.js room without camera parallax. Missing-WebGL and context-loss modes leave the room area empty against night while preserving ordinary semantic project links.

## Elevation & Depth

Borders, surface changes, the hero's bounded perspective camera, and restrained CSS chapter plates establish hierarchy. Depth is structural rather than ornamental: the Three.js camera belongs only to the spatial room, while shallow CSS fold/plate transitions support chapter handoffs. Neither may control text legibility or navigation. Small shadows may support controls; do not add glow, heavy image filters, or unrelated faux-3D effects.

## Shapes

Structural frames, controls, inputs, and cards have square corners. Badges are also square. Curves are limited to recognizable icons or explicitly documented structural line junctions, never generic controls.

## Borders & Outlines

Use one-pixel shared rules without doubled boundaries. The top edge of each non-hero homepage chapter is one shared exception: a one-pixel mineral line, a five-pixel night gap, and a six-pixel mineral line. Its three dimensions are centralized as chapter border tokens. Focus uses a two-pixel offset ring with a three-pixel gap: paper on dark/accent surfaces, night on paper/mineral. Fields use accessible outline borders, not the decorative outline-variant. Never hide focused controls or essential information behind hover.

## Components

### Actions and fields

Navigation uses ordinary anchors. In-place actions use buttons. Both share canonical visual variants and at least 44px targets. Inputs, textarea, select, and checkbox controls have visible labels and associated help/error text; mobile form text is at least 16px. Pending, disabled, invalid, empty, and success states keep honest descriptions. Contact processing is simulated; it does not send mail or persist leads.

### Images and content

Dotted background fields use the shared `DotTexture` component and `dot-texture.css` across homepage chapters, inner-page title fields and rails, catalogs, project drawing fields, Contact information, and closing posters. All instances match the Services reference: a 0.75px paper dot fading at 1px, 32% paper contrast, and one viewport-based repeat of `clamp(9.75px, 1.17vw, 17.55px)` with the same phase. Route styles must not override the pattern. The independent animated film-grain layer is unchanged.

Original catalog media and metadata are preserved. Technical plans, sections, and diagrams use containment and a full-resolution link. Service capabilities use a typed registry of original normalized line icons; project photography remains reserved for the portfolio and hero.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep original images recognizable, upright, and naturally colored.
- Preserve all authored copy, statistics, specifications, contact details, and project destinations.
- Reuse production primitives in this guide and visitor pages.
- Keep visible keyboard focus and native scrolling.

### Don't

- Do not add a theme selector, custom shader spectacle, another WebGL scene, scroll hijacking, or fabricated project measurements. The single approved Three.js hero renderer is the bounded exception; New Work remains Canvas 2D.
- Do not shrink interactive targets below 44px or crop technical drawings.
- Do not use arbitrary component colors, broad transition-all rules, or unrelated stock imagery.
- Do not link this guide from public navigation or the footer.
